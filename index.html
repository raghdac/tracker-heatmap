Tracker Heatmap Benchmark
Undergraduate thesis · results seminar · draft results

Which tracker gives the most faithful customer heatmap?

One detector, three trackers, the same videos. This page replays the saved outputs of the experiment, so every box, heatmap and number shown here is the one reported in the thesis.

Method

Only the tracker changes

Every configuration reads the same cached YOLOv8n detections and feeds the same heatmap procedure. A detection-only baseline skips the tracker, so the gain from tracking can be measured against it.

Findings

What the results say

BoT-SORT leads, by a small margin

Combined HOTA is 0.200 for BoT-SORT, 0.194 for ByteTrack and 0.176 for DeepSORT in the crowded scenario, and 0.391, 0.351 and 0.384 with a moving camera. ByteTrack falls behind when the camera moves, because it has no camera-motion compensation.

Tracking improves the heatmap only slightly

Frame-weighted SSIM is 0.683 without any tracker and 0.702 with the best one. DeepSORT (0.682) does not improve on the baseline. A better tracking score does go with a better heatmap, but the differences are small.

The detector limits everything

At 640 px the trackers recover about a fifth of the people in the crowded scenes. At 1280 px recall rises to about a half, SSIM to 0.88–0.89 and the count error falls from about 60% to about 22%. The tracker order stays the same.

Scope

What these results do not show

  • Each scenario has three sequences that meet the selection criteria. With three sequences the comparison is descriptive; no claim of statistical significance is made.
  • MOT17 and MOT20 are pedestrian benchmarks, not shop recordings. The retail scenario of the thesis is still to be evaluated.
  • YOLOv8n is used off the shelf. A detector trained on crowded scenes would change the absolute scores, and possibly the size of the gaps.
  • Tracker settings are the defaults of the library version used, the same starting threshold for every tracker, with no tuning per tracker.
